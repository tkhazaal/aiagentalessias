## 1. Your role

**The whole business, in one picture.** Everything is simplified into one front
door: the community. Ads bring people to the **$89 community**. From there,
everyone we talk to is already a member. Some stay and grow; the right ones move
up to the premium program. One entrance, then a path upward.

Your job sits at the heart of it: **get new members activated, keep them
engaged, and move the right ones toward the premium.** Everything downstream
depends on what happens in a member's first days, and that's your ground.

**The big shift: reactive → proactive.** The old way noticed someone only when
they were already cancelling. The new way gets a member genuinely engaged in
their first week so they never reach the cancel point.

Why it matters (the psychology): almost no one cancels because the content is
bad. They cancel because they never really started. They joined in a burst of
hope, then life got loud, the pain felt too big to touch, and they quietly
drifted. The only real lever is at the beginning, before the drift starts.

### What you own vs. what Tania owns

- **You own:** Activation, engagement, and retention. Running the weekly
  orientation call. Catching wins and at-risk signals. Gathering testimonials.
  Setting (qualifying) members for the premium and handing warm, qualified
  people to Tania.
- **Tania owns:** The offer itself, the closing calls, final creative direction,
  and the pitch words and slides you'll use.

The mindset: you're not a support inbox waiting for problems. You're the person
who makes a new member feel seen in their first 48 hours, so they actually
begin.

## 2. Your one number

**7-day activation rate:** the % of new members who, within their first 7 days,
finish the entire Start Here in the classroom, post once, and get a reply from
us.

This is the number at the top of your scorecard, **not churn**. Churn is the
lagging result; activation is the leading cause you can move today.

### A member is "activated" when they've done all three

1. **Finished the entire Start Here** in the classroom (Calm Start plus the full
   5-part video series, not just one lesson).
2. **Made one post** (an intro or their situation; initials are fine).
3. **Gotten one reply back** from you or Tania.

> Note: the Start Here is growing — the 5-video series is being added to it.
> "Activated" means a member completed the **whole** Start Here, not just Calm
> Start Lesson 1. Always point new members to finishing the entire Start Here.

Why these three: each is a psychological threshold. Finishing the Start Here =
they invested real time and absorbed the frame. Posting once = they stopped
being a spectator and became a participant (identity follows action). Getting a
reply closes the loop — they feel seen by a real person, the single strongest
reason people stay anywhere. Miss any one and the member is still on the fence.

### How to calculate it

For any given week's new members:
**(number who hit all three within 7 days) ÷ (total who joined that week) × 100.**

**Worked example:** 20 people joined the week of the 1st. By day 7, 9 finished
the Start Here, posted, and got a reply. Activation rate = 9 ÷ 20 = **45%**.
Next week you're trying to beat 45%.

### How to track it (keep it simple)

Start manual, not a dashboard. A simple spreadsheet you click through — one row
per new member, columns for joined date, finished Start Here, posted, got a
reply, activated (Y/N).

1. Update it during your daily Power Hour — minutes if daily, hours if it piles
   up.
2. Each week, calculate the cohort's rate and bring it to the Growth & Retention
   meeting.
3. Once you see patterns, feed them into your second brain ("here's what I'm
   noticing, what should I try?"). Manual first, insights second, automation
   later.

**Secondary scorecard:** your 90-day goal — we measure your starting activation
rate first, then set a target together. Moving the number up from where it
starts is the win.

## 3. Your weekly rhythm

### Every day: the Activation Power Hour

One focused hour, same time every day, aimed entirely at members in their first
7 days. The most important hour of your day. Do it first, before the inbox pulls
you into reacting.

**Run it in this order:**

1. **Pull this week's new members** — the people currently inside their 7-day
   window. They come first.
2. **Sort them into three buckets:** not started (no Start Here, no post),
   started but not posted, and posted. Each bucket gets a different move.
3. **Not started:** send a specific, personal DM (below) and tag them under a
   prompt post so posting feels easy.
4. **Started, not posted:** nudge with a concrete, low-effort ask ("just initials
   and two sentences").
5. **Posted:** reply personally, by name, with real guidance — the reply that
   activates them, the highest-leverage thing you do all day.
6. **Scan for wins and at-risk signals** as you go, and log them (wins → section
   6; at-risk gets a same-day reach-out).
7. **Update your activation sheet** so the number stays live.

**Don't** spend the Power Hour answering general questions or moderating old
threads. That's reactive work — it feels productive but doesn't move your number.
Protect this hour for the 7-day window only.

> **Power Hour DM — not-started member**
> "Hi [name], I saw you joined this week, so glad you're here. I don't want you
> to feel lost, so just one thing to start: what's the hardest part of this for
> you right now? Even a sentence helps me point you to the right place."

> **Power Hour nudge — started but not posted**
> "[name], you've made a start, that's the hard part. The one thing that changes
> everything here is posting your situation, even just initials and two
> sentences. Want to drop it under [this week's prompt post]? I'll personally
> reply with a first read."

### Every week

- Run the weekly onboarding & orientation call (section 4).
- **Cancel block (1 hour, batched):** send the exit form, tag the top 3 cancel
  reasons, bring the patterns to the weekly meeting. You're learning who stays
  and why, not chasing everyone back.
- **Skool Churn Insights** (skootcrm.com/skool-churn-insights): get it set up and
  shared if it isn't already, and use it weekly. It surfaces cancel reasons
  straight from Skool, so we still learn why someone left even when they never
  replied to the exit email.
- **Update the retention tracker with Riaz.**

Why the cancel block is batched and calm: chasing every cancel one-by-one is
draining and rarely works — they've usually decided. Batching it once a week
turns it into research: spotting patterns you fix at the entrance.

### Seven days a week: responsiveness

The community can't go dark, weekends included. Our parents often have the most
time to post and reach out on weekends, so silence then costs us members. This
isn't full weekend days — it's a short daily check-in so members always hear
back and nothing at-risk slips.

**A sustainable weekend rhythm:** a 15–20 minute check-in each weekend morning —
scan new members, at-risk signals, and any direct questions. Acknowledge
anything that matters (a question, a struggling member, a win) so no one waits
until Monday. The ones that matter never slip.

The mindset: you're not trying to save everyone at the exit. You're getting more
people to truly start at the entrance. That's where the game is won.

## 4. The onboarding & orientation call

Your biggest deliverable. One call, every week, that welcomes, orients, teaches,
and softly points to the application. It does two jobs: orients brand-new members
so they know how to use the community, and works like a weekly webinar that
teaches and points the right people to the application.

### Full run of show

- **First ~10 min — Welcome + orientation.** Warm welcome, then genuinely walk
  them through the community (the part people skip, and why members get lost):
  - Where the Start Here is, and that they should finish the whole thing this
    week.
  - How to add the weekly calls to their calendar (show them, don't just say it).
  - Where to post, and where to ask questions.
  - That they can message you (Alessia) directly, and that you actually reply.
- **~5 min — Wins (if any).** Invite a couple of recent wins. For very new
  members there may not be many yet, so pull from the wider community, a recent
  member win, or a short story of your own. Doubles as our testimonial engine, so
  record it (with permission).
- **~20 min — The training.** Real, valuable teaching — the reason they showed
  up. Pure value, no selling. Teach one useful thing they can act on this week.
- **Last ~5 min — The soft pitch, to the application.** Tania gives you these
  words + slides: "That was the general advice. If you'd like advice specific to
  your exact situation, here's how we do that," then point to the application.
  **You pitch to the application, you don't close.**

Why orientation comes first: a confused member is a churned member. Two minutes
showing them where things live, and that a real person is on the other end, does
more for retention than an hour of content. Never assume they'll "figure it out."

**If attendance is low or no one has wins yet:** low attendance is normal early.
Run it fully anyway and record it — the recording becomes an asset new members
watch later. No member wins yet? Use one of your own, a past member's, or a story
from the wider community. Always record (with permission).

**Who builds what:** Tania gives you the pitch section word-for-word plus those
slides. You build and own the welcome, orientation, wins, and training. Tania
approves your first version, then it's yours to run and improve each week.

**Build the deck in Claude** — fast and on-brand. Slide structure to start from:

1. **Welcome:** who we are, why you're here, what this call is.
2. **How the community works:** the one front door, what your membership gives
   you.
3. **Your first steps:** finish the entire Start Here (Calm Start plus the 5-part
   series).
4. **How to navigate:** where to post, where to ask questions, how to message
   Alessia, where the calls live.
5. **Add the calls to your calendar:** the weekly call time, with the link.
6. **A recent win:** one story that shows what's possible.
7. **The training:** 2–4 slides of real teaching.
8. **Want specific help?:** the soft pitch to the application (Tania's words and
   slides).

**After the call:** post the recording where members can find it, DM anyone who
registered but didn't attend with the replay, and follow up personally with
anyone who engaged with the pitch. (DMing the no-shows is a big one — they still
need to see the pitch.)

### Every week: the welcome new members post

Separate from the call, post a weekly welcome post in the community. It makes new
members feel seen and pulls them toward the call and classroom.

- Tag, by name, the members who joined in the last 7 days.
- Link the orientation replay so they can catch it if they missed it.
- Share a couple of recent wins (from the Wins tab, a member's story, or the
  wider community).
- Invite them to watch the replay and message you anytime.

> **Weekly welcome post**
> "Welcome to our newest members this week, [@tag each new member]! So glad
> you're here. If you missed our orientation call, catch the replay here: [link].
> And a couple of wins from the community lately: [Lisa just shared ..., Kim
> shared ...]. Jump into the Start Here, post your situation, and message me
> anytime, I'm here for you."

Why this beats just posting the replay: tagging them by name makes them feel they
belong on that call, so they're far more likely to watch it, engage, and see the
pitch. New members get both this post and their welcome DM.

## 5. The first week: the Golden Path

The exact touchpoints that turn a new member from spectator into participant.
Each day has a job, a script, and a fallback for when they don't respond.

- **Day 0 — Welcome (email + thank-you page).**
  > "Welcome. There are only two things to do this week: finish the entire Start
  > Here in the classroom, and post your situation using this template. Even
  > initials and a couple of sentences is enough. That's it, I'll be looking for
  > your post."

  Why: reduce it to two clear actions. Overwhelmed people do nothing; a tiny,
  obvious next step gets them moving.

- **Day 1 — Personal DM.**
  > "Hi [name], instead of a generic welcome, can you reply with 1–2 sentences:
  > what's the hardest part of this for you right now?"

  Why: the goal is to get them to type anything back. The first reply is the
  hook. **Fallback (no reply by Day 2):** "No pressure at all, even one word
  about where things stand helps me help you."

- **Day 3 — Incentive to act.**
  > "Post your situation using this template by Friday and I'll get you a personal
  > reply with a first read and some wording to try."

  Why: a small deadline plus a promised reward overcomes the fear of posting.
  **Fallback:** offer to post on a call together or let them send it to you
  privately first.

- **Day 7 — Gentle re-engage.**
  > "If you haven't finished the Start Here or posted yet, I get it, it usually
  > means the pain feels big and it's scary to touch. That's normal. If you're
  > willing, just reply 'start' and I'll give you the smallest first step."

  Why: name the real reason (fear, not laziness) and shrink the ask to a single
  word. Removing shame re-opens the door.

> **The "situation post" template you give members**
> "[Initials or first name] here. My situation: [one line, e.g. my adult daughter
> stopped speaking to me 2 years ago]. What I've tried: [one line]. What I'm
> hoping for: [one line]."

**Worked example — a member's first week:** Day 0: Maria joins, gets the welcome
email. Day 1: you DM the "hardest part" question; she replies "my son blocked me
and I don't know why." Day 2: you reply warmly, point her to Start Here Lesson 1,
invite her to post. Day 3: she hasn't posted — you send the "post by Friday"
nudge. Day 4: she posts using the template; you reply personally, by name, with
one concrete thing to try. Day 6: she's finished the Start Here. **Activated.**
That Day-4 reply is the whole game.

Not everyone needs every touch. If a member is already posting and getting
replies, they don't need all four DMs — but still check: **did they watch the
orientation call or the replay?** If not, message them ("I noticed you weren't on
the orientation call, did you get a chance to watch the replay?") — that's where
they see the pitch. The specific touches depend on what they have and haven't
done.

**Don't** send the same generic "let me know if you need anything!" to everyone.
It reads as automated and gets ignored. Every touch names them and references
their actual situation.

## 6. Catching wins (the milestone moment)

When a member has a breakthrough, that's the moment to move them deeper and get a
testimonial. Right after a win they're flooded with hope and gratitude and at
their most open. Wait a week and the feeling cools — **speed is everything.**

**What counts as a win:**

- **Movement in the relationship:** "my daughter texted me back," "we spoke for
  the first time in a year," "she agreed to meet."
- **An internal shift:** "I finally stopped begging and it changed everything,"
  "I feel calm for the first time."
- **An engagement win:** showed up to a call, posted vulnerably, or helped
  another member.

**The sequence: win → testimonial → set**

1. Spot it during your Power Hour or daily scan.
2. React warmly in the moment; publicly celebrate, then reach out.
3. Get the testimonial while the feeling is fresh (section 8).
4. Qualify them gently in the DMs, including the money question (section 7).
5. If qualified, set a call with Tania. If not, you've still gained a testimonial
   and a warm relationship. Nothing is wasted.

> **Milestone outreach**
> "Thank you so much for sharing that! Tania would love to hear more about your
> story and how things have been going. Could I grab 15 minutes with you this
> week?"

**Worked example:** A member posts "My son called me on his own for the first
time in 8 months." You comment publicly ("This is huge, thank you for sharing it
here"), then DM the milestone outreach. On the 15-minute call you capture a
testimonial, then gently ask the qualifying questions. She's a fit and open to
going deeper, so you set a call with Tania. One post became a testimonial and a
warm set.

**Don't** jump straight from "congrats" to "want to buy the premium?" It feels
transactional and kills the moment. Celebrate first, capture the story, qualify
gently, and only then talk about the next step — in that order.

## 7. Setting & qualifying

**You set, Tania closes.** Your job is to make sure only qualified people reach
her calendar.

**Start here: the application is your qualifying script.** Go through the
application end to end and read every question. Tania keeps it current, and you
always work from that live version. The application questions are exactly what
you qualify for in the DMs — once you know them cold, you already know how to
qualify.

### Your part of the CLOSER framework (the "set")

You own the first three letters. Tania owns the rest.

- **C — Clarify:** understand where they are and why they reached out now.
- **L — Label:** name what they're dealing with, so they feel understood.
- **O — Overview:** overview past pains — what have they already tried that
  didn't work.

_Tania takes it from there: Solidify, Explain away concerns, Reinforce — the
close._

**Set conversation, in the DMs:**

- **Clarify:** "Tell me a bit about what's going on with [child's
  name/situation], and what made you reach out now?"
- **Label:** "So it sounds like [reflect it back: you've been carrying this for
  years and you're exhausted from trying to fix it alone]. Is that right?"
- **Overview:** "What have you already tried to reconnect, and how did that go?"

Why you reflect before you offer anything: people open up to someone who
understands them, not someone who's selling. Objections live in the vague — the
more specific you get them, the smoother the path.

### The financial qualify (before anyone books)

> **Money question**
> "Based on what you told me, I think you might be a great fit for our next thing,
> and I'd get you a call with Tania. Just so we don't waste anyone's time, the
> next step is an investment, around **$4K–$15K** to keep working with us. Is
> that something you'd be totally against?"

Not against it → book. "Yes, against it" → "Totally understand, no worries" →
don't book. Say it calmly and without apology; the ease in your tone tells them
it's normal. "Totally against" is deliberately soft — it's easy to say "no, not
against it," which is all we need.

**Qualify before the onboarding call too:**

> **Pre-onboarding message**
> "To make sure we give you as much value as possible, we have a few quick
> questions to understand your situation before your onboarding call. Please fill
> this out."

_(Coming soon, Tania is building this with Jesse: a short application before the
onboarding call that auto-qualifies green / yellow / red. Not live yet.)_

### What disqualifies (don't book these)

- Flatly against the investment range.
- Wants to start "someday," not now (see the booking rule).
- Looking for free ongoing coaching, not ready to commit.
- Not the decision-maker and can't involve them.

**Booking rule (BANT):** anyone who says they'd want to start **more than a week
from now** isn't ready to buy — don't book them. We only book people ready to
move.

### The hand-off to Tania

> **Set summary for Tania**
> ```
> Name: [name] · Situation: [one line]
> What they've tried: [one line] · The win/trigger: [what prompted this]
> Money question: [not against it] · Ready to start: [this week/soon]
> ```

## 8. Collecting testimonials

Proof is what makes the ads and VSL work. Our ads and VSL only work because real
parents tell their stories. Treat gathering it as an ongoing habit, not a
one-off.

**When to ask:** always at a win — the moment a member shares a breakthrough,
never when they're still in the hardest part. A happy member says yes easily;
someone still struggling feels put on the spot.

**The mindset: ask forgiveness, not permission.** You do not ask "is it okay if
we use this?" — that hands them an easy "let me think about it." The right to use
it is already handled by the disclaimer members agree to when they join (Skool's
terms / our Start Here). So you assume usage. What you ask for is only to **share
their story**, framed around helping the next parent.

**On the 15-minute call, exactly how it goes:**

1. The whole call **is** the testimonial. Frame it as a warm check-in to
   celebrate their progress, and record it. Spend most of it drawing out their
   story — that's your raw material.
2. At the very end (reciprocity is highest after you've given your time), ask for
   the usable "moments."
3. Only if they hesitate do you reframe with best-case / worst-case. Otherwise
   capture it and move on.

> **The ask, end of call (help-others frame)**
> "This has been so good to hear. So many parents are exactly where you were,
> feeling completely alone and sure it's too late. Your story could be the very
> thing that helps one of them believe it's possible. Would you share a couple of
> moments with me so another parent can hear it?"

Never say "can we use this in our marketing?" — that single question kills the
yes.

> **If they ask "what do you mean, for another parent to hear it?"**
> "There's a chance another parent who's thinking about the community sees your
> story, and it's exactly what they needed to believe change is possible for them
> too."

> **Only if they raise a privacy worry (say it once)**
> "I hear you. Just so you know, we only ever advertise to parents, so your child
> won't see it. But let's play it out. Best case, they never see it and another
> parent gets their child back because of you. Worst case, they do see it, and
> they find out you cared enough to work this hard on your relationship. Would
> they really think that's a bad thing?"

If they still want it kept private, take it down — no friction; it happens rarely
so don't build a heavy approval process. Anonymous is fine too: voice off-camera,
first name only, or change the name.

**How hard to push:** push hard on the **meaning** (their story could help
another parent get their child back). Stay **gentle on the format.** Offer a
ladder so no one feels forced — any rung is a win:

- Full video (best) → First name only → Voice only → Written → Anonymous

**The moments to capture (ask these in order):**

1. "Take me back to before — what was the hardest part, and what were you doing
   to try to fix it?"
2. "What made you decide to join / take the jump?"
3. "What shifted for you here, and when did you first notice it?"
4. "What's different in your relationship now?"
5. "What would you say to a parent who's where you were?"

Have them **repeat the question** before they answer ("The moment I knew things
had changed was...") so each clip stands on its own without your voice in it.

**Organize what you collect:**

- Save every testimonial in one place (a shared folder), labeled by name, format,
  and core result.
- Note the strongest line from each — those become ad and VSL hooks.
- Bring new testimonials to the weekly meeting so the team knows what proof we
  have.
- **Permission:** covered by the disclaimer in Skool's Start Here. If anyone is
  ever uncomfortable later, listen to their concern first and take it down if
  they still want (rare).

## 10. Handling hard moments

Warmth first, then a clear path. Escalate anything you're unsure about.

- **An upset or angry member.** Lead with acknowledgement, never defense.
  > "I hear you, and I'm really sorry this has felt frustrating. Let me make it
  > right. Can you tell me exactly what happened so I can fix it for you?"

  Then actually fix it, or flag to Tania if it's beyond you. Follow up once
  resolved. **Don't** explain, justify, or match their energy.

- **A refund or cancel threat.** Stay calm and curious, not panicked.
  > "Totally understand, and I want to make sure this was worth it for you. Before
  > anything, can I ask what you were hoping would be different? If there's
  > something we can fix or a step I can walk you through, I'd love to, and if
  > not, I'll take care of it for you."

  Genuine problem you can solve → solve it. **Payment/access/refund language →
  flag to Eti/Tania per the dispute-risk rule.** Never argue someone out of a
  refund.

- **Someone venting or trauma-dumping.** Hold space briefly and warmly, then
  guide to a concrete next step. You're a compassionate guide, not their
  therapist.
  > "Thank you for trusting me with this, it sounds incredibly heavy and you're
  > not alone in it here. The best next step I can give you is [Start Here lesson /
  > a specific post / a call]. Let's start there together."

- **A no-show to a call they booked (warm re-book, no guilt).**
  > "Hi [name], we missed you, life happens. I still saved a spot for you. Want me
  > to grab you a new time this week?"

- **An over-needy member (endless DMs).** Kind, with a boundary back to the
  community.
  > "I love how committed you are to this. The best place for questions like this
  > is the community, post it there and you'll get support from me and from other
  > parents who've been exactly where you are. I'll keep an eye out for it."

- **Sensitive or crisis language (escalate).** Anything mentioning self-harm, a
  member in crisis, or a safety concern goes to **Tania right away** — don't
  handle it alone. Same for dispute-risk language (refund, chargeback, "fraud,"
  "unauthorized"): acknowledge kindly and flag per the escalation rule.

The rule of thumb: acknowledge the person first, solve or guide second, and
escalate the moment it's beyond your lane. Nobody expects you to have every
answer — they expect you to be human and not let it drop.

| **7-day activation rate** (primary) | % of each week's new members who finish the Start Here, post, and get a reply within 7 days. From your activation sheet. |
| **Monthly churn %** | Cancellations ÷ active members. Trending down, toward under ~10–12%. From the retention tracker + Skool Churn Insights. |
| **Responsiveness** | Nothing that matters sits unanswered, weekends included. Watched via the community and your daily check. |
| **Testimonials** | A steady flow each month, logged in the testimonials folder. |
| **Qualified sets** | The right members moved from the community into the application / a call with Tania. |

We measure your starting activation rate first, then set targets together and
review them in the weekly Growth & Retention meeting.

**The weekly self-review (5 minutes):**

- What was my activation rate this week, and did it move?
- Did anything important slip, and why?
- What win or at-risk pattern did I notice?
- One thing I'll improve next week.

**What a strong 60–90 day review looks like:**

- **The number moved:** activation rate clearly up toward the target we set.
- **The standard held:** the community stayed responsive seven days a week,
  nothing important slipped.
- **You built systems:** Golden Path touchpoints, templates, and the orientation
  deck running smoothly and improving.
- **You owned the community's health:** wins surfaced, at-risk members caught
  early, qualified sets sent to Tania.
- **Self-learning & initiative:** actively getting better at the craft, bringing
  ideas Tania didn't ask for, and improving this playbook as you learn.

**Ownership: the real bar.** Completing the checklist is not the job. Owning the
numbers is. "I messaged everyone" is not done. Done is "the numbers are moving,
and if they're not, here's what I'm changing." The muscle to build is thinking
outside the playbook, owning your lane end to end, spotting what's not working,
and fixing it without waiting to be told.

## 12. What good looks like

**Winning:**

- New members activate in their first week.
- The community stays responsive seven days a week.
- You surface wins and at-risk signals in real time.
- Only qualified people reach Tania's calendar.
- A steady flow of testimonials.
- You know your activation rate every week.

**Red flags:**

- Energy going to cancels instead of activation.
- The community going dark on weekends.
- Members joining and going silent, unnoticed.
- Unqualified people booking Tania's time.
- Generic "let me know if you need anything" DMs.
- Bringing cancel stories but not the activation number.

**If churn isn't improving, ask two questions:** (1) Am I doing enough on the
front end (touches, replies, follow-ups)? (2) Are we even bringing in the right
people? If you see a pattern — say the last 30 who joined and left barely engaged
— bring it to Jesse: "I don't think we're bringing in the right type of member."

**Test and learn.** A message not landing? Try a different one. Getting great at
the craft — activation, churn, testimonials — is part of the role.

**Flag content gaps, don't create them yourself.** If a specific need shows up
over and over (say a divorced-parent / dealing-with-an-ex theme, 15+ times in 30
days), bring it to Tania and she'll record a short training.

**The through-line:** your job is to get more people to truly start in their
first 7 days, and to move the ones who are ready toward Tania. Do that
consistently and everything downstream works.
